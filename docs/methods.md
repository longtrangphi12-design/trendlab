# Methods

All evidence below comes from synthetic data generated by
`trendlab.simulate`. Nothing in this repository has been run on real data.

## 1. Trend detection (`trend.py`)

**Mann-Kendall.** For a series x_1..x_n, S is the sum of sign(x_j - x_i)
over all pairs i < j. Variance under "no trend" is
`[n(n-1)(2n+5) - sum t(t-1)(2t+5)] / 18`, where t counts tied values.
Z uses a continuity correction (S-1 or S+1); the two-sided p-value uses the
normal approximation. NaNs are dropped; fewer than 3 valid points gives NaN.

**Sen's slope.** Median of all pairwise slopes (x_j - x_i)/(t_j - t_i).
Robust to isolated outliers. Unit: *data unit per time step* (the library
does not know whether a step is a month or a year).

**Assumptions and limits.**
- Values are assumed independent over time. Positive autocorrelation makes
  p-values too small (section 3).
- The normal approximation is rough for very short series.
- Seasonal cycles violate independence; aggregate or split by season first
  (`cleaning.py`: `select_months`, `yearly`).

## 2. Many cells at once (`multitest.py`)

Running one test per grid cell yields many false positives: with 2000
independent trend-free cells and alpha = 0.05, about 100 cells are expected
to look significant by chance.

- **Bonferroni:** p * m (capped at 1). Controls the chance of *any* false
  positive. Strict.
- **Benjamini-Hochberg (BH):** controls the expected share of false
  discoveries among flagged cells. Valid for independent or positively
  dependent tests.
- NaN p-values are ignored and do not count toward m.

**Limit.** Correcting for many cells does not repair wrong p-values in
individual cells.

**Dependence.** Neighbouring cells are likely positively correlated, the setting BH is designed for in theory; the guarantee is cleanest for one-sided tests, and with correlated cells the number of false discoveries varies more from map to map. Treat this as an assumption, not a proven property of the data. Benjamini-Yekutieli is the stricter option, valid under any dependence.

## 3. Autocorrelation warning flag (`autocorr.py`)

This flag does **not** correct p-values. It marks cells where the
independence assumption is doubtful.

- Lag-1 autocorrelation r1 of each cell's series, after removing the Sen
  line (so a real trend is not mistaken for autocorrelation).
- A cell is flagged when r1 exceeds the 95th percentile of r1 measured on
  independent series of the same length, computed the same way
  (`simulate.autocorr_null_threshold`). By design, about 5% of independent
  cells are flagged.
- This is *temporal* autocorrelation inside each cell, not spatial
  correlation between neighbours.

**Reading the flag.** No flag does not mean "independent": the flag misses
part of the autocorrelated cells (tables below).
The flag also cannot tell a real trend from a chance drift (section 4.5).

## 4. Evidence from synthetic data

Setup: grids of independent AR(1) series with no trend (unit variance),
2000 cells (40 x 50), no spatial correlation, one random seed per table.

### 4.1 False positives of Mann-Kendall (n = 24), share of cells flagged

| phi | raw p <= 0.05 | after BH |
|-----|---------------|----------|
| 0.0 | 0.035 | 0.000 |
| 0.2 | 0.070 | 0.000 |
| 0.4 | 0.146 | 0.000 |
| 0.6 | 0.265 | 0.081 |

Autocorrelation inflates false positives, and BH cannot fix this (8.1% of
cells still flagged at phi = 0.6 although no trend exists).

### 4.2 Bias of r1 (mean over cells)

| phi | n | r1 with detrend | r1 without detrend |
|-----|-----|-----------------|--------------------|
| 0.0 | 24 | -0.078 | -0.042 |
| 0.0 | 48 | -0.041 | -0.020 |
| 0.0 | 100 | -0.017 | -0.007 |
| 0.0 | 200 | -0.007 | -0.003 |
| 0.6 | 24 | 0.377 | 0.451 |
| 0.6 | 48 | 0.491 | 0.529 |
| 0.6 | 100 | 0.551 | 0.568 |
| 0.6 | 200 | 0.578 | 0.585 |

r1 underestimates phi on short series; the bias shrinks roughly as 1/n.
Detrending adds a smaller extra bias that also shrinks with n.

### 4.3 Calibrated flag: share of cells flagged

| n | threshold | phi = 0.0 | phi = 0.2 | phi = 0.4 | phi = 0.6 |
|-----|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| 24 | 0.226 | 0.049 | 0.234 | 0.529 | 0.797 |
| 48 | 0.183 | 0.051 | 0.385 | 0.837 | 0.986 |
| 100 | 0.135 | 0.056 | 0.667 | 0.988 | 1.000 |

The threshold depends on n and must be recomputed for each series length.
The n = 24 default in `simulate.py` is only a placeholder.

### 4.4 Ground-truth experiment on one grid

Setup: 20 x 25 grid (500 cells), AR(1) noise with phi = 0.4, n = 24, a real
trend of 0.15 per step added to 100 cells (the first four latitude rows),
one random seed, processed with `report.analyze`.

| | inside block (100 cells, real trend) | outside (400 cells, no trend) |
|---|---|---|
| raw p <= 0.05 | 99 | 65 |
| BH significant | 94 | 30 |
| flag (detrended r1, threshold 0.236) | 52 | 198 |

- BH returned 124 cells, 30 of them outside the block: 24.2% false
  discoveries against a nominal 5%.
- 16.3% of the trend-free cells have raw p <= 0.05.
- The detrended flag is as frequent inside the block (52%) as outside
  (49.5%): a real trend does not inflate it.
- The top-ranked cells by p-value show slopes of 0.15 to 0.21 against 0.15
  injected: ranking by p favours cells whose noise inflated the slope.

### 4.5 Can the lag-1 flag screen out false discoveries? No

Same grid. For cells outside the block (no real trend):

| r1 measure | threshold | flagged | BH false among flagged | not flagged | BH false among not flagged | real-trend cells flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| detrended | 0.236 | 198 | 9 | 202 | 21 | 52 of 100 |
| not detrended | 0.270 | 227 | 25 | 173 | 5 | 98 of 100 |

Reading (a hypothesis consistent with the data, not a proof): a false trend
usually comes from a persistent series drifting in one direction. Removing
the Sen line also removes that drift, so the detrended flag is blind to it.
Without detrending the flag sees the drift but also flags almost every real
trend. With 24 points a real trend and a persistent drift look alike, so no
flag computed from the series alone can separate them.

Limits of this experiment: one seed, small counts (9 and 21), independent
cells, AR(1) noise.

Consequence for reporting: a cell that is significant after BH carries an
uncertainty at small n that the flag does not remove. A flagged cell means
weaker evidence, not a false result; an unflagged cell is not safe.

Draft map legend: "Flag: in these cells a year tends to resemble the previous
year more than in random data of the same length, so the trend result may be
less reliable. No flag does not mean the result is safe: the check catches
only part of such cases and cannot tell a real trend from a chance drift."

### 4.6 Negative result: trend-free pre-whitening (TFPW)

Variant tested (`autocorr.tfpw_mann_kendall`): remove the Sen line, measure r1
on the residual; only if r1 exceeds the null-calibrated threshold (0.236 in
this experiment), pre-whiten the residual, add the trend back and run
Mann-Kendall. n = 24, trend-free 40 x 50 grids, one seed.

Share of cells with p <= 0.05 (raw / after BH):

| phi | plain | TFPW |
|-----|-------|------|
| 0.0 | 0.035 / 0.000 | 0.036 / 0.000 |
| 0.2 | 0.070 / 0.000 | 0.077 / 0.000 |
| 0.4 | 0.146 / 0.000 | 0.171 / 0.000 |
| 0.6 | 0.265 / 0.081 | 0.326 / 0.172 |

Ground-truth grid of section 4.4: BH significant inside the block 94 -> 95 of
100, outside 30 -> 40 of 400 (false share 0.242 -> 0.296).

Diagnosis (phi = 0.6): 1560 of 2000 cells were pre-whitened. False-positive
share was 24.2% -> 31.9% among pre-whitened cells and 34.8% among the 440
cells that were not pre-whitened. 159 cells moved to false positive and 38
moved away.

Reading (hypotheses consistent with the data): (1) the cells prone to false
trends are drifting series whose detrended r1 is low, so they are the least
corrected; (2) pre-whitening shrinks the noise around the Sen line while the
added-back slope stays, so a chance slope looks stronger.

Decision: TFPW is not used in the pipeline and p-values stay uncorrected.
Limits: one seed, independent cells, AR(1) noise, n = 24, one rule for
choosing which cells to pre-whiten. Other corrections (variance correction of
the Hamed-Rao type, block bootstrap) were not tested.

## 5. Known limitations and open items

- Single random seed per table; repeat with many seeds before quoting
  precise percentages.
- Simulated cells are independent; real neighbouring cells are correlated,
  so false positives will cluster and their count will vary more.
- The null model is AR(1); real series may differ.
- No correction of p-values for autocorrelation (only a warning flag); a
  TFPW variant was tested and made false positives worse (section 4.6).
- `io.validate` does not catch a grid that repeats longitude -180 and 180
  (duplicate column after standardisation).
- The flag threshold uses the full series length; cells with fewer valid
  points are flagged slightly more easily than designed (the threshold
  falls as n grows: 0.226, 0.183, 0.135 for n = 24, 48, 100). The warning
  "many gaps" appears only below 80% valid points.
- `yearly` mean tolerates missing steps but does not check which steps are
  missing; losing one part of the seasonal cycle biases the mean.
- Seasons that cross the new year are not supported by `yearly`.
- Not yet written: thresholds, grid weighting, spatial statistics,
  validation against reference data, plotting.

## 6. Reproducibility

Every simulation is seeded (`seed` argument). Tests in `tests/` check the
formulas against hand-calculated values.
